# User Research Skill

## Trigger
Use when asked to: create a "user research plan", write an "interview guide", set up a "usability test", design a "survey", form "research questions", or when help is needed with any aspect of understanding users through research.

## Goal
Plan, design, and execute rigorous user research — from defining questions to selecting methods to building guides and analyzing results.

## Process

### 1. Define the Research Question
Start by answering:
- What decision does this research need to inform?
- What do we already know? What are we assuming?
- What would change our direction if we learned it?

Avoid: "We want to learn everything about users." 
Write a focused question: *"Why are users dropping off during the onboarding flow after step 2?"*

### 2. Choose the Right Method

| Goal | Best Method |
|------|-------------|
| Understand motivations, mental models, workflows | User Interviews |
| Test if users can complete specific tasks | Usability Testing |
| Measure attitudes, preferences at scale | Survey |
| Understand what users do (not say) | Behavioral Analytics |
| Discover problems early with minimal users | Cognitive Walkthrough |
| Validate a specific hypothesis quickly | A/B Test |
| Understand support/complaint patterns | Support Ticket Analysis |

### 3. Research Plan Template
```
Research Plan: [Project Name]
Date: [Date]
Researcher: [Name]

Research Question:
[Primary question this study will answer]

Hypotheses:
1. We believe [assumption]
2. We believe [assumption]

Method: [e.g., 45-minute moderated user interview]
Participants: [n=X, criteria: role, experience level, etc.]
Timeline: [Recruitment by X, sessions X–X, synthesis by X]
Deliverables: [Report, insights deck, video highlights]
```

### 4. Screener Survey
Used to recruit the right participants:
```
1. What is your job title / role?
2. How often do you [relevant behavior]?
   [ ] Never  [ ] Monthly  [ ] Weekly  [ ] Daily
3. Have you used [product/category] in the last 6 months?
4. [Disqualifier question if needed]
```
Target: recruit people who match your user persona, not people who are "good at giving feedback."

### 5. Interview Guide Template
```
Introduction (5 min)
- Thank you for your time. We're here to learn, not to test you.
- There are no right or wrong answers.
- We may take notes / record — is that okay?
- Do you have any questions before we start?

Warm-Up (5 min)
- Tell me a bit about your role and what a typical day looks like.
- How do you currently [relevant task/domain]?

Core Questions (25–30 min)
[Topic 1: Current Behavior]
- Walk me through the last time you [task]. What happened?
- What tools or methods do you use for [task]?
- What's the most frustrating part of that process?

[Topic 2: Needs & Goals]
- What would make [task] significantly easier for you?
- If you had a magic wand, what would the ideal experience look like?

[Topic 3: Context & Motivation]
- Why does [task] matter to you / your team?
- What happens when things go wrong with [task]?

Prototype / Task Section (if usability test)
- "I'm going to give you a scenario and ask you to try to complete a task."
- Scenario: [realistic task description]
- Think aloud — tell me what you're seeing, thinking, and feeling.

Wrap-Up (5 min)
- Is there anything we didn't cover that you think is important?
- Who else should we talk to?
- Any questions for us?
```

### 6. Usability Test Protocol
```
Task 1: [Task name]
Scenario: "[Realistic user scenario in plain language]"
Success Criteria: [What does success look like?]
Metrics: Completion rate, time on task, error count, satisfaction (1–7)
Observer notes: [space for noting moments of confusion, hesitation, delight]
```

### 7. Survey Design Principles
- Open with easy, non-threatening questions
- Ask one thing per question
- Use 5 or 7-point Likert scales (not 10-point)
- Put open-ended questions at the end
- Limit to 10 minutes max (15–20 questions)
- Pilot with 3 people before sending

### 8. Running Sessions
- Recruit 5–8 participants per distinct user segment for interviews
- Use the "5-second test" rule: if you must explain the question, rewrite it
- Take verbatim notes on quotes — paraphrase observations
- Note body language and hesitations, not just words

## Output Structure
1. **Research Plan** — question, method, participants, timeline
2. **Screener** — recruitment criteria and questions
3. **Discussion Guide / Test Protocol** — ready-to-use guide
4. **Survey** — complete question set (if applicable)
5. **Analysis Framework** — how you'll code and synthesize the data
6. **Deliverables Plan** — what the output will be and who it's for
